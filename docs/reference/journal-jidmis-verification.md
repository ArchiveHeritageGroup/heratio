# Journal verification - JIDMIS (Journal of Intelligent Decision Making and Information Science)

**Summary / verdict (11 Aug 2026).** JIDMIS is **not an outright fake** - it is genuinely Scopus-indexed with a Scimago source record - but the **call-for-papers flyer soliciting submissions is misleading, and this journal is NOT recommended** for The AHG / Dr Pieterse's publication record. The disqualifying issue: the flyer advertises "Open Access - No Publication Fee," while the journal's own website states an **APC of USD 1,200**. Combined with WhatsApp/Gmail solicitation, an unknown publisher, one issue per year, and very recent (volatile) indexing, the right call is to **pass**. "Scopus-indexed" does not mean reputable.

## What checks out (real)
- eISSN **3079-0875**; DOI prefix `10.59543/jidmis`; runs on OJS 3.x.
- Publisher: **Nexora Academic Press**.
- Has a **Scimago/Scopus source record**; journal reports **Scopus CiteScore (2026) 3.4** and **SJR 0.771** - consistent with the "Scopus" and "0.77" on the flyer.
- Open access, CC-BY 4.0; claims acceptance rate 17%, final decision ~75 days.

## Red flags (why to avoid)
1. **Fee misrepresentation (disqualifying):** flyer says "No Publication Fee"; the journal's own site charges **APC USD 1,200**. A CFP that hides a $1,200 charge is not trustworthy.
2. **Solicitation via WhatsApp (+91) and Gmail** (`ijcallforpaper@gmail.com`), not the official OJS submission system - classic aggressive-recruitment pattern.
3. **Brand-new, unknown publisher; one issue/year; very recently indexed** - CiteScore/SJR are provisional and volatile; Scopus de-lists many such titles. "In Scopus now" is not "in Scopus in two years."
4. **"Q1 Ranked" badge unverified** - Scimago blocked automated checking. SJR 0.771 is plausibly Q1 in a broad category, but treat the badge as an inflated flyer claim until confirmed on the Scopus source list.
5. Generic OJS boilerplate (e.g. "form approved by the Rector of the University") suggests a templated setup.

## If ever reconsidered - the only checks that matter
- Look it up **directly on `scopus.com/sources` by ISSN 3079-0875** for the real, current quartile (not the flyer/Scimago mirror).
- Confirm whether it is on the **DHET-accredited list** (almost certainly not yet - too new) for any SA subsidy value.
- Both must be a solid yes; even then, the flyer's fee misrepresentation is reason enough to walk.

## Principle (reusable)
Scopus/Scimago indexing is necessary but not sufficient. Predatory or low-quality journals can and do appear in Scopus. Verify: (a) real quartile on the Scopus source list by ISSN, (b) DHET accreditation for SA subsidy, (c) whether solicitation is via official OJS vs WhatsApp/Gmail, (d) whether the flyer's fee/indexing claims match the journal's own site. A mismatch on fees is a hard stop.
