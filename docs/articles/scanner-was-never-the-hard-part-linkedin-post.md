# LinkedIn post - The Scanner Was Never the Hard Part

We audited a "finished" heritage digitisation delivery. 6,351 files, boxes closed, invoice paid.

Then we looked properly.

2,375 were duplicates. 6,342 had no searchable text, though OCR was in the contract. Over 12,000 images were below the required resolution. Thousands of "preservation masters" were 1-bit files that had thrown the tonal detail away for good.

Open any single file and it looks fine. The failure was in the aggregate, and in the things nobody was asked to check.

That is the pattern every time. The scanner was never the hard part.

Scanning is solved. The hard 80% is what the image passes through afterwards: completeness reconciled to an inventory, no duplicates (checksums, not eyeballs), effective ppi measured not claimed, real lossless masters, a text layer that is actually tested, metadata that links to the arrangement.

And most of those defects were not the operator's fault. They were written into the spec. A contract that never asks for de-duplication or a text-layer test gets a delivery without them.

Don't buy scanning. Buy the acceptance criteria, and the per-batch verification that proves them.
