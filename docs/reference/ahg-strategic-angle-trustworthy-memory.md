# AHG strategic angle - trustworthy memory as infrastructure (v2)

**Summary.** The AHG's emerging strategic positioning, developed in a partner brainstorm (16 Aug 2026). The hook is **"provenance is the new scarcity"**; the category (v2 refinement) is **"trustworthy memory is becoming critical infrastructure"**; the AHG position within it is **"sovereign, verifiable memory."** The real prize is a single **organising idea** that collapses the AHG's service list into one thesis: *everything we do helps institutions preserve, govern and prove memory.* Working memo (Fable-rewritten for human voice): `heratio/stuff/AHG-strategic-angle-brainstorm.md` (+ .docx). This note is internal strategy - keep the provocations un-sterilised until they become a public article or board paper.

## The core insight (v1 hook)

AI made *having* information free, so content stopped being evidence. What is now scarce and appreciating is the opposite of content: **provenance** - proof of what a record is, where it came from, who may use it, and that it has not been altered. Archives are the only institutions that have done provenance, chain of custody and authenticity as their core craft for centuries; that craft is about to become the trust infrastructure the AI economy runs on. We are not in the archive-software business; we are in the trust business.

## The inversion nobody is pricing in

The sector worries about AI *consuming* archives (training on collections, the GEO invisibility problem). That is real but small. The bigger shift: as AI is regulated (EU AI Act and successors) and AI outputs carry legal/financial/reputational weight, every serious AI system will need to **ground and cite** its claims in authoritative, provenanced sources. The archive stops being a passive store and becomes an **active trust endpoint that AI systems query and must cite**. Whoever owns the machine-queryable provenance layer for a society's records owns something structural. The category is empty.

## v2 refinement: widen the room, keep the hook

Provenance alone is too narrow to be the destination - it is **one (load-bearing) component of a larger territory: trustworthy memory.** The fuller trust stack is provenance + context + custody + integrity + authority + rights + sovereignty + community governance + preservation + accountability. So:
- **Lead with provenance as the wedge** (it is the component AI/regulation just made economically scarce *and* machine-verifiable - it has a market logic and a technical hook).
- **Expand into "trustworthy memory as critical infrastructure" as the category** (the room to occupy).
- Do not go abstract too early, or the category reads as a mission statement rather than a winnable fight.

**The real prize is the organising idea.** Today the AHG looks like a list (AtoM, Heratio, records management, digital preservation, digitisation, AI, hosting, risk management, consulting). One sentence turns the list into a company with a thesis: **everything the AHG does helps institutions preserve, govern and prove memory.**

## Angles that fall out of it

1. **The Trust API for memory (flagship):** the archive as a queryable provenance/authenticity service (C2PA, RiC, PREMIS under the hood) that AI systems, courts and journalists call to ground and verify claims. Provenanced answers, not search results.
2. **Authenticity-as-a-service:** the verification layer that defends the real record against AI-fabricated history (forged struggle-era photos, "lost" recordings).
3. **Sovereign AI memory:** AI on your own data, jurisdiction and rules; the AHG already does the hard parts (in-jurisdiction, pluggable sovereignty, indigenous-protocol-aware) that hyperscalers structurally cannot.
4. **The Memory Commons:** a governed shared utility where pluggable modules keep each member's sovereignty, so small institutions / municipalities / the Global South get real preservation collectively. A credit union for memory - and the AHG is literally the Digital *Commons* Group.
5. **The Right to Remember:** memory justice as a rights framework (the world spent a decade on the right to be *forgotten*; for the marginalised the deeper injustice is erasure). A mission no B2B vendor can fake.

## The blind spot in our own story

Our thesis is that governance is the hard part and the technology is solved. If we believe that, then the 95th software package is not the moat - it may be the trap. If value sits in governance, method and trust, the real product is the **protocol and the trust authority**, and the app is downstream. TCP/IP won the internet, not the routers. **OpenRiC is us stumbling toward exactly this without naming it.** The bigger move: stop being archive-vendor-number-seven competing with AtoM, and become **the standard - the trust protocol for memory - that other people's software has to implement.**

## The discipline

The category earns the right to be believed only if anchored by one concrete proof. Big frame, small demo: one collection an AI grounds and cites, with the provenance chain attached. The slogan without the endpoint is just a better slogan.

## Three cheap first moves

1. **Plant the flag** - the flagship essay (*Provenance Is the New Scarcity* / *The Archive Is Becoming an API That AI Must Trust*), opening on provenance and landing on trustworthy memory as infrastructure. Next in the governance blog series, and the biggest yet.
2. **Prototype one Trust API endpoint** - a provenanced, machine-queryable, citable collection an AI grounds against.
3. **Name the protocol** - reframe OpenRiC (or a thin layer above it) as the open trust-and-provenance protocol for memory, and pursue adoption with two or three institutions and one AI/regulatory party.

## Stop saying / start saying

- **Stop:** "archival management system," "AtoM successor," "digitisation toolkit," "records management."
- **Start:** "the trust layer for institutional and cultural memory," "provenance is the new scarcity," "in an age of infinite content, we make memory you can prove."
