# LinkedIn post - Provenance Is the New Scarcity

There is a hard drive on my desk with ten thousand photographs. Some are real. Some I generated this morning, in minutes, for nothing.

You cannot tell which is which. Neither can I.

That breaks something we have relied on for the entire history of recorded information: that a document, a photo, a recording is *evidence* of something.

For thirty years, value sat in *having* information. AI made having it free. And the moment content became infinite, content stopped being evidence.

So what is scarce now? The opposite of content: provenance. Proof of what a record is, where it came from, who may use it, and that no one has altered it.

Archives have done exactly this - provenance, custody, authenticity - as their core craft for centuries. We mislabelled it as backward-looking compliance. It is about to become the trust infrastructure the AI economy runs on.

The twist most people are missing: as AI gets regulated and its outputs carry legal weight, AI systems will have to *cite* authoritative, provenanced sources. The archive stops being a passive store and becomes the endpoint AI must check itself against.

Provenance is the new scarcity. Trustworthy memory is what we build with it.
