# Professional indemnity and public liability: what The AHG must declare

Date: 2026-08-27
Author: Dr Johan Pieterse
Status: proposal form part-completed and with the underwriter's questions answered; financial and claims declarations outstanding

A professional indemnity and public liability proposal form was completed for The AHG. This note records what the underwriter asks for, what our answers are, and - more usefully for anyone who does this again - the exposures this class of form does not ask about but which matter most to a business like ours.

## The governing principle

The form states plainly that the insurance contract is based on the information given, and that material information withheld can affect indemnity. That reframes the exercise: this is not paperwork, it is the definition of what will and will not be paid out. Every temptation to round a description down in order to hold the premium down is a temptation to create the gap that voids a future claim.

That principle also sets the boundary for what may be answered on someone's behalf. Company registration details, addresses, qualifications and a description of the business are matters of record and can be completed from the company profile. Financial figures, claims history, warranties and choices of cover are declarations. They belong to the signatory and must never be inferred.

## What the form asks

The structure is consistent across this class of proposal, and knowing it in advance saves a round trip:

- **Identity**: legal entity name exactly as it should appear on the policy, postal and physical addresses, subsidiaries, registration and VAT numbers, date of commencement, professional and regulatory body memberships.
- **People**: number of principals, qualified professional staff and others; a table of principals with qualifications, institution, date qualified and years of experience.
- **Services**: a business description the contract will rest on, and a split of work by activity as a percentage of total fees.
- **Geography**: any work performed outside the country, or where the end product is used elsewhere, with the percentage of fees per territory.
- **Risk management**: formal staff training, a formal quality and risk system and whether it is externally reviewed, a named risk officer, use of legal risk services, whether sub-consultants are appointed directly by the client and carry their own insurance, use of standard contracts, and how often liability is limited in writing.
- **Self-assessment**: your own top three risk areas likely to give rise to a claim. This is not a formality; it is read.
- **Claims and insurance history**: five years of claims, circumstances that might become claims, employee dishonesty, and whether any insurer has declined, imposed special terms or cancelled.
- **Finances**: fee income across three financial years, net of sub-consultant fees. Audited statements may be requested.
- **Cover**: limits of indemnity, retroactive cover, and optional extensions.

## Our answers, and the gaps

Identity, people and services could all be completed from the company record. The business description given covers the full range honestly: archival arrangement and description, records-management consulting, digitisation and digital preservation, book and paper conservation, custom software development and data migration, hosting and support of web-based archival applications, training, and AI-assisted tools - for galleries, libraries, archives, museums, government, universities and private organisations.

Two structural facts about the entity are worth remembering because they surface repeatedly in procurement as well as insurance. The company was incorporated recently, so its trading history as a legal entity is short even though its principals' experience is long. And the principals' qualifications and decades of practice sit with the people rather than the entity, which is exactly what a professional indemnity underwriter is being asked to price.

Everything financial and every declaration was left for the signatory.

## The three exposures worth arguing about

This is the part worth keeping. The form's own questions do not surface the risks that actually characterise this business.

**Physical custody of irreplaceable originals.** The largest single exposure is not professional advice at all. Digitisation and conservation work means taking possession of clients' original, often unique documents and heritage objects. This is a care, custody and control exposure - bailment - and standard office contents cover does not respond to a damaged or destroyed client original. The proposal form never asks about it directly. It must be raised with the broker as an explicit question, and if the answer is that it is excluded, that exclusion is the gap to close before anything else.

**Work in other territories.** Any work performed abroad, or whose end product is used abroad, has to be declared with a share of fees. Where that includes North America, cover for claims brought in those jurisdictions is almost always a separate extension carrying additional premium, and is commonly excluded by default. Listing the country in one answer does not buy the cover; the extension has to be requested.

**Hosting and data.** Running software as a service and holding client data creates two distinct exposures that the form treats as optional extensions: cyber liability for unauthorised or malicious third-party access, and cover for restoring documents - our own or a third party's - that are damaged, lost or stolen. For a business built on custodianship of records, and operating under privacy legislation, these read as near-essential rather than optional. Regulatory enquiry costs belong in the same conversation.

## The recurring lesson

An insurance proposal is one of the few documents where being comprehensive about what you do is in your own interest rather than against it. Underwriters price uncertainty; a vague description invites a broad exclusion. Describe the physical custody, the hosting and the international work fully, then negotiate the extensions that cover them.
