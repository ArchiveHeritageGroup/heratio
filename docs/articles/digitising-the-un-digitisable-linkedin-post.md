# LinkedIn post - Digitising the Un-Digitisable

A digitisation programme is approved. It covers the paper, and it is going well.

Down the corridor sit the things it quietly ignored: reel-to-reel tapes, VHS cassettes holding the only record of interviews with people now dead, cans of 16mm film, a carved mask, a drawer of coins. None fit under a scanner, so none were scoped.

And of everything the institution holds, these have the least time left.

Magnetic tape decays and sheds its signal. Acetate film gets vinegar syndrome and consumes itself. The machines to play them are no longer made, and the engineers who align them are retiring. A fragile tape may survive one last play, so the transfer has to be right the first time.

Objects are not flat either: a photograph throws away the depth, the true scale, the surface. They need geometry, colour and scale together, with a colour chart and a scale bar in every shot.

The standards exist (IASA, FADGI, CARARE, 3D-ICONS). The failure is that the spec was written for flat paper, and the material with the shortest clock was scoped last.

For paper, a deferred decision costs a year. For a 40-year-old tape, it is the recording you will never hear again.
