# Calm and Axiell Collections: what the migration rumour actually is

Date: 2026-08-26
Author: Dr Johan Pieterse
Status: desk research from public sources; the decisive source (the UK user group) is closed and unread

A rumour reached us that the company behind **Calm**, the archive collections management system widely used in UK repositories, is in trouble and that its clients are shopping for alternatives. The rumour has a real event behind it, but the framing is wrong in a way that matters for how we approach it commercially.

## The event behind the rumour

**Axiell, which owns Calm, is actively moving Calm customers onto a different product, Axiell Collections.** This is not speculation - it is a published programme:

- A dedicated **Calm to Axiell Collections Transition** webinar series: one-hour sessions teaching Calm users to perform their familiar work - cataloguing, linking, searching, import and export, administration - in Axiell Collections instead.
- A standing **Upgrading to Axiell Collections** programme, including sessions covering "the next steps for Calm software and the move to Axiell Collections - the migration process, potential costs, timelines."

That last phrase is the tell. Costs and timelines are the vocabulary of a planned migration, not an optional upgrade. When a vendor systematically teaches its customers to use a different product, those customers reasonably conclude the current one is finishing.

A third-party ecosystem has formed around it: at least one UK consultancy now markets Calm-to-Axiell-Collections migration as a service. Consultants build practices around movements that are actually happening.

## What is NOT established

**No end-of-life date has been published.** Axiell's own transition material stops short of saying Calm is discontinued, gives no end-of-support date, and does not say whether the move eventually becomes mandatory. It refers those questions to a named contact rather than answering them publicly - itself informative, but not the same as an announcement.

**Calm is still being developed.** Version 9.3 introduced Linked Data functionality, promoted as a first for UK commercial collections management software. That is not the behaviour of a dead product.

**There is no sign of company distress.** Axiell has been acquiring rather than shedding - an established collections software company, and a Norwegian library vendor. A media division was divested, explicitly to concentrate on the libraries, archives and museums business. Revenue is modest, on the order of tens of millions of dollars annually, spread across more than a thousand library organisations and several thousand archives and museums. That profile describes a small consolidating vendor, not a failing one.

## The correct reading

This is **post-acquisition product consolidation**, not collapse. A vendor that has bought several overlapping collections systems is rationalising onto one platform. It is an ordinary and predictable corporate move.

The consequence for Calm sites is the same either way, and this is the part that matters: **they face migration cost and disruption regardless of how satisfied they are with their vendor.** The switching cost they have spent years avoiding has become unavoidable.

## What this means commercially

The opening is real, and it can be made honestly.

**Do not pitch vendor distress.** It cannot be evidenced, and it would cost credibility instantly with anyone who knows the vendor well - which in this sector is most senior archivists.

**Do pitch the decision point.** Your vendor is asking you to migrate anyway. That makes this the moment to decide *where* you migrate to, rather than defaulting to whichever product the vendor has chosen for you. Institutions facing a forced platform change are the most receptive prospects in any market, because the objection that normally ends the conversation - "we are not going through a migration" - has already been overcome by someone else.

A migration from Calm suits us structurally. Calm is built on the same international descriptive standards our platform implements, and archival data migration is our core competence rather than an adjacent service.

## The gap in this research, and how to close it

The decisive source is closed to us. The **UK CALM User Group** is a moderated mailing list for archivists and system managers, and its stated purpose is to discuss issues "that are not appropriate for sharing with the supplier." That is precisely where candid sentiment about the transition lives, and it is members-only, not web-indexed. Its website is also currently offline.

One conversation with a working Calm user would be worth more than all of the above. Three questions to ask:

1. Has the vendor given you a date?
2. What is the migration being quoted at?
3. Are you treating this as an upgrade, or as a procurement decision?

The third question is the commercially decisive one. An upgrade has no competitor; a procurement decision does.

Also worth periodically re-checking whether an end-of-life date has since been published. The material behind this note may age quickly.
