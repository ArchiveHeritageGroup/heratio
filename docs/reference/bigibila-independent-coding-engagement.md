# Independent methodological coding: the Bigibila engagement

Date: 2026-08-28
Author: Dr Johan Pieterse
Status: practice stage in progress; formal reliability sample sealed and not yet opened

Bigibila Governance Archaeology is an Indigenous-led research study in Australia. Dr Johan Pieterse has agreed to act as an **independent methodological coder** for its reliability stage. This note records the shape of that role, the boundaries around it, and several lessons that transfer well beyond this one engagement.

**Confidentiality note.** The research pack is controlled material issued under a confidentiality and ICIP declaration. This note therefore records the *method and the process*, not the content: no evidence text, no sample identifiers, no codebook definitions, no reference outcomes, and nothing about the specific Nations, places or ancestral material in the corpus. Anyone needing the substance must go to the pack itself, under the declaration.

## What the role actually is

The study rests on a written codebook of governance functions, developed against a frozen corpus of observations across three registers. The reliability question is not whether an outside reader agrees with the researcher's conclusions. It is narrower and more useful: **can the written methodology be picked up by someone with no access to the researcher's classifications and applied consistently?**

That distinction governs everything about the role. Disagreement is a valid result. Recording that no function is present, or that the evidence is insufficient, is a valid result. Forcing a match would destroy the very thing the exercise measures. Agreement is subsequently analysed by percentage agreement and Cohen's kappa, function by function, with disagreement patterns examined rather than smoothed away.

The engagement is deliberately walled off from any platform, proof-of-concept or commercial discussion with the same party. That separation is what makes the methodological review worth anything, and it is worth insisting on in any comparable arrangement.

## Independence controls worth reusing

The study's own controls are well constructed and worth borrowing wholesale for any inter-rater work:

- The coder must not seek or receive the researcher's original classifications before returning their own.
- A sealed formal sample, opened only on the researcher's confirmation, after a practice stage.
- A practice set that is explicitly excluded from the reliability statistic, carrying a reference key intended to be opened only after all practice decisions are saved.
- The returned formal workbook preserved in its original state before any comparison or reconciliation.
- Structural fields - identifiers, evidence text, row order, sheet names, controlled metadata - locked against alteration; only authorised coder fields may be touched.

## A hard lesson: bulk-reading a workbook destroys blind review

The most transferable finding came from a mistake. Opening the practice workbook with a spreadsheet-reading tool returned **every sheet in one response, including the reference key**. No warning, no opt-in - a single read call, and the answers were on screen.

For the practice stage this was tolerable: that key is designed to be revealed afterwards, and the practice set carries no weight in the statistic. Had the same call been made against a sealed formal instrument, it would have silently voided the coder's independence, and the damage would have been invisible in the returned workbook.

**The rule that follows: when handling a blind or sealed research instrument, never bulk-read it.** Enumerate the sheets first, then read only the sheet you are entitled to see. Treat "read the whole file" as an unsafe default whenever a file is structured to withhold part of itself from the reader. The same caution applies to sealed tender responses, examination material, and anything else where the container deliberately separates what you may see from what you may not.

This is also the boundary for tool-assisted work generally on this engagement: assistance with workbook mechanics and controlled fields is fine, involvement in the classifications is not.

## Two methodological points with wider value

**Fix the single-code versus multi-code convention before measuring anything.** Where a classification scheme has functions that legitimately overlap, a coder recording the single dominant function and a coder recording every applicable function will diverge on a large share of items *while reading the evidence identically*. The resulting statistic then understates the instrument rather than testing it. This convention must be settled in the protocol, not left to the coder, and it is the first thing to check in any reliability design.

**Distinguish absence in the world from absence in the record.** Two superficially similar outcomes do quite different analytical work:

- *No function present* - the evidence is adequate, and it establishes that the thing being looked for is not there.
- *Insufficient evidence* - the thing may well exist, but this particular record cannot establish it.

One is a finding about the subject; the other is a finding about the source. Coders who do not share that distinction will split inconsistently between the two, and both categories usually carry analytical weight.

That distinction is not confined to research coding. It is the same judgement archival appraisal makes constantly - whether a gap in a collection reflects something that never happened or something that was never recorded - and it is worth naming explicitly in any appraisal, audit or classification methodology we write.

## Sequencing, and why it matters

The practice stage exists to surface definition questions before the formal sample is opened. The correct order is: complete and save the practice decisions first, *then* raise definition questions, so that the coder's own reading is formed before any discussion of definitions influences it. Raising questions first is not fatal, but it is less clean, and where a study is measuring independence the cleaner sequence costs nothing.

Deliverables were therefore split: a timeline commitment sent immediately, and the definition questions held until the practice outcomes were recorded.
